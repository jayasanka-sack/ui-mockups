About

A community since 2004. A non-profit since 2012.

OpenMRS began in 2004 when Burke Mamlin and Paul Biondich of the Regenstrief Institute, working with AMPATH in Eldoret, Kenya, decided that the medical record system they were building should be shared rather than owned. Hamish Fraser of Partners In Health joined them that September. What started as one system for one HIV programme became a global community and, in 2012, a non-profit to hold it.

Mission

To improve health care delivery in resource-constrained environments by coordinating a global community that creates a robust, scalable, user-driven, open-source medical record system platform.

Vision

Every person, everywhere, served by a health system that has the information it needs to care for them.

Values

  • Open. Code, data standards, decisions and mistakes, all in public.
  • User-driven. Built with the clinicians and implementers who depend on it.
  • Shared. Improvements go back to everyone; nobody pays a licence.
  • Durable. Stewarded by a non-profit, not sold by a company.

How it's organised

Four parts, each with a job.

The community does the work. OpenMRS Inc. holds the assets, employs a small support team, and raises the money. A board governs the Inc.; squads and a leadership team set technical direction in the open.

Timeline

Twenty-two years, dated.

The milestones we can source. More will be added as they're verified.

A brief history, on the wiki →
  1. 2004 Burke Mamlin and Paul Biondich (Regenstrief) start building a shared medical record system with AMPATH in Eldoret, Kenya. Hamish Fraser (Partners In Health) joins at MedInfo in September.
  2. 2006 First public release of the OpenMRS platform. First implementations outside Kenya.
  3. 2007 OpenMRS joins Google Summer of Code; it has taken part every year since.
  4. 2010 The community decides to form a non-profit rather than a company.
  5. 2012 OpenMRS Inc. is registered as an Indiana non-profit.
  6. 2014 OpenMRS Inc. receives US 501(c)(3) status, 7 March.
  7. 2021 A peer-reviewed study finds OpenMRS implementations in 62+ countries and calls it the most broadly deployed EHR in low- and middle-income countries.
  8. 2023 Verified as a Digital Public Good, 13 January.
  9. 2024–25 National systems in Kenya, Uganda and Cambodia move to OpenMRS 3. OMRS24 in Nairobi; OMRS25 in Uganda.
  10. 2026 OpenMRS 3.8.0 ships with 639 pull requests from 9 organisations.
